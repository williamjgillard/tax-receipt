    Why this isn't the $547.3B headline number: Ottawa's own Annual Financial Report puts total federal expenses at $547.3B for FY2024–25 — that's full accrual accounting, and it includes $53.4B in accrual-based debt charges and $4.0B in net actuarial losses (pension re-valuations: a real accounting entry, but not money paid to any person or vendor). The $474.4B here is the near-cash "budgetary expenditures" basis — what was actually paid out — which is why every dollar on this page can be traced to a department, a spending type, and often a named vendor or recipient. Neither number is wrong; they answer different questions.

      Public Services and Procurement Canada and Shared Services Canada are central buyers: much of what shows up under their names (building leases, IT infrastructure, bulk contracts) was actually procured on behalf of other departments, not spent on themselves — a large PSPC or SSC number reflects "who signed the contract," not "who used the thing."
      What's genuinely untraceable to a name, by law: individual salaries (privacy), and individual benefit payments like OAS, EI, and the Canada Child Benefit (paid to millions of people, never published per-person). Spending below the $10K contract / $25K grant disclosure threshold also has no public paper trail. Everything else here is real, sourced, government-published data.
