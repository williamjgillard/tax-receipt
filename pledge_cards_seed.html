Prime Minister Mark CarneyCommitted March 14, 2025
Eliminate the consumer carbon levy.
  • March 14, 2025Sworn in as prime minister; directs the consumer fuel charge cut to $0/tonne by regulation.
  • April 1, 2025Consumer carbon levy on gasoline and home heating drops to $0 nationwide.
  • March 12, 2026Bill C-4 (Making Life More Affordable for Canadians Act) receives royal assent, formally repealing Part 1 of the Greenhouse Gas Pollution Pricing Act, retroactive to April 1, 2025.
Sources: Prime Minister of Canada, swearing-in announcement; CBC News; Department of Finance Canada, royal assent notice. The industrial carbon-pricing system for large emitters is separate and was not part of this commitment — it remains in place.
Prime Minister Mark CarneyCommitted June 9, 2025
Hit NATO's 2%-of-GDP defence spending target — five years ahead of the prior 2032 goal.
  • June 9, 2025Carney announces Canada will meet the 2% target within the 2025–26 fiscal year, roughly $9B in new spending that year.
  • June 2025, NATO Hague SummitCanada joins NATO allies' new pledge to invest 5% of GDP annually by 2035 (3.5% core defence + 1.5% broader security).
  • March 26, 2026Carney announces Canada achieved the 2% target for fiscal 2025–26, citing over $63B spent that year — the largest year-over-year defence increase in generations.
  • Target: 2035The follow-on 5%-of-GDP pledge has no confirmed interim milestones yet.
Sources: CBC News, June 9, 2025; Prime Minister of Canada, March 26, 2026.
Government of Canada, Department of FinanceCommitted May 2025
Cut the lowest federal personal income tax rate from 15% to 14%.
  • May 2025Government announces the cut, applying to the first $57,375 of taxable income for roughly 22 million Canadians.
  • July 1, 2025New 14% rate takes effect (a blended ~14.5% for the partial 2025 tax year).
  • March 12, 2026Formally legislated via Bill C-4's royal assent.
  • 2026First full calendar year at 14%; individual savings grow to roughly $500/year, up to $840 for two-income families.
Sources: Department of Finance Canada, May 2025; Department of Finance Canada, royal assent notice.
Government of Canada, Housing & InfrastructureCommitted September 2025
Launch Build Canada Homes and accelerate affordable housing construction.
  • September 2025Build Canada Homes launches as a new federal housing agency, mandated to build, finance, and catalyse affordable housing.
  • November 2025Federal budget commits $13B (cash basis) over five years to the agency.
  • February 2026~100 days in, the agency reports agreements representing over 7,500 homes across Ottawa, Winnipeg, and Nunavut.
  • April 2026Eight projects approved for 1,100+ rental homes in Ottawa; combined Direct Build and partnership agreements exceed 10,000 homes.
  • July 2026A second phase of the Build Canada Homes–Québec partnership is confirmed, adding roughly 2,000 more affordable homes.
  • No date setThe government's original goal of nearly doubling annual homebuilding by decade's end has no disclosed interim target — housing analysts have flagged the lack of measurable benchmarks as of mid-2026.
Sources: Housing, Infrastructure and Communities Canada, January 2026; Housing, Infrastructure and Communities Canada, July 2026; Policy Options, May 2026.
Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship CanadaTabled November 4, 2025
2026–2028 Immigration Levels Plan — cut temporary resident admissions, hold permanent resident admissions near current levels.
  • November 4, 2025Plan tabled: permanent resident admissions set at 380,000/year through 2028; new temporary resident arrivals targeted at 385,000 in 2026, then 370,000 in 2027–28 (down from 367,750 work permits and 305,900 study permits in 2025); explicit goal of temporary residents under 5% of the population by end of 2027.
  • Target: early–mid 20272026's actual admission numbers won't be confirmed against the plan's targets until IRCC publishes year-end figures.
Source: IRCC, Supplementary Information for the 2026–2028 Immigration Levels Plan.
