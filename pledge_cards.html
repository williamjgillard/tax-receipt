Government of Canada / Prime Minister Mark CarneyCommitted November 4, 2025
Balance the federal government's day-to-day operating budget by fiscal 2028–29.
  • November 4, 2025Budget 2025 sets two new fiscal anchors: balancing the operating budget (day-to-day spending, excluding capital investment) by 2028–29, and a declining deficit-to-GDP ratio.
  • September 15, 2026At the Canada Investment Summit, Carney announces Ottawa is now on track to balance the operating budget a year early, in fiscal 2027–28, citing a 10% year-over-year jump in federal revenues.
  • September 24–25, 2026The Parliamentary Budget Officer publishes an analysis disputing that timeline, projecting the operating budget won't actually balance until 2029–30 — a year later than the original target and two years behind Carney's claim — and flagging that the government's operating-vs-capital spending classifications are inconsistent.
  • Target: 2027–28 (government) / 2029–30 (PBO)Which projection is correct won't be known until actual fiscal year-end results are reported.
Sources: CBC News, Budget 2025 date announcement; The Hub, September 21, 2026; The Hub, September 25, 2026; The Globe and Mail, September 2026.
Government of CanadaCommitted September 21, 2026
Compress major federal project reviews to “one project, one decision, in one year” via the Building Canada Strong Act.
  • September 21, 2026Bill C-39, the Building Canada Strong Act, receives first reading — a coordinated federal review process meant to compress a historically five-year approvals timeline (assessments, permits, and consultations run concurrently) into a single federal decision within a legislated one-year window, alongside changes to federal labour relations aimed at reducing transportation-sector strikes.
  • September 23–25, 2026Bill C-39 is debated at second reading in the House of Commons; the government moves a motion to refer it to committee, but the House has not yet voted on that motion or begun committee study.
  • No date setBill remains subject to Parliamentary debate and amendment; royal assent and the first project actually reviewed under the new one-year standard haven't happened yet.
Sources: Government of Canada, news release, September 21, 2026; Parliament of Canada, LEGISinfo, Bill C-39; Bloomberg, September 21, 2026; openparliament.ca, Bill C-39 debates.
Prime Minister Mark CarneyCommitted March 14, 2025
Eliminate the consumer carbon levy.
  • March 14, 2025Sworn in as prime minister; directs the consumer fuel charge cut to $0/tonne by regulation.
  • April 1, 2025Consumer carbon levy on gasoline and home heating drops to $0 nationwide.
  • March 12, 2026Bill C-4 (Making Life More Affordable for Canadians Act) receives royal assent, formally repealing Part 1 of the Greenhouse Gas Pollution Pricing Act, retroactive to April 1, 2025.
Sources: Prime Minister of Canada, swearing-in announcement; CBC News; Department of Finance Canada, royal assent notice. The industrial carbon-pricing system for large emitters is separate and was not part of this commitment — it remains in place.
Prime Minister Mark CarneyCommitted June 9, 2025
Hit NATO's 2%-of-GDP defence spending target — five years ahead of the prior 2032 goal.
  • June 9, 2025Carney announces Canada will meet the 2% target within the 2025–26 fiscal year, roughly $9B in new spending that year.
  • June 2025, NATO Hague SummitCanada joins NATO allies' new pledge to invest 5% of GDP annually by 2035 (3.5% core defence + 1.5% broader security).
  • March 26, 2026Carney announces Canada achieved the 2% target for fiscal 2025–26, citing over $63B spent that year — the largest year-over-year defence increase in generations.
  • Target: 2035The follow-on 5%-of-GDP pledge has no confirmed interim milestones yet; the Parliamentary Budget Officer estimates reaching it will require roughly $33.5B/year in additional defence spending on average through 2035, a fiscal plan the government says will come in the fall budget.
Sources: CBC News, June 9, 2025; Prime Minister of Canada, March 26, 2026; Parliamentary Budget Officer, fiscal implications of the NATO 5% commitment.
Government of Canada, Department of FinanceCommitted May 2025
Cut the lowest federal personal income tax rate from 15% to 14%.
  • May 2025Government announces the cut, applying to the first $57,375 of taxable income for roughly 22 million Canadians.
  • July 1, 2025New 14% rate takes effect (a blended ~14.5% for the partial 2025 tax year).
  • March 12, 2026Formally legislated via Bill C-4's royal assent.
  • 2026First full calendar year at 14%; individual savings grow to roughly $500/year, up to $840 for two-income families.
Sources: Department of Finance Canada, May 2025; Department of Finance Canada, royal assent notice.
Government of Canada, Housing & InfrastructureCommitted September 2025
Launch Build Canada Homes and accelerate affordable housing construction.
  • September 2025Build Canada Homes launches as a new federal housing agency, mandated to build, finance, and catalyse affordable housing.
  • November 2025Federal budget commits $13B (cash basis) over five years to the agency.
  • February 2026~100 days in, the agency reports agreements representing over 7,500 homes across Ottawa, Winnipeg, and Nunavut.
  • April 2026Eight projects approved for 1,100+ rental homes in Ottawa; combined Direct Build and partnership agreements exceed 10,000 homes.
  • July 2026A second phase of the Build Canada Homes–Québec partnership is confirmed, adding roughly 2,000 more affordable homes.
  • September 8, 2026Build Canada Homes partners with Manitoba and the City of Winnipeg to ramp up construction of at least 820 housing units (740+ affordable) in Winnipeg and Swan River, with up to $104.7M in BCH funding; construction expected to begin spring 2027.
  • September 9, 2026Build Canada Homes and the Government of Alberta announce a partnership to build at least 1,460 affordable homes across 21 projects, with Ottawa providing up to $220M and Alberta up to $165M.
  • September 22, 2026Build Canada Homes launches the $1.5-billion Canada Rental Protection Fund, aimed at helping non-profit and co-op housing providers acquire and preserve at-risk rental buildings — up to 7,000 units over the fund's first five years; application intake begins October 2026.
  • No date setThe government's original goal of nearly doubling annual homebuilding by decade's end still has no disclosed interim target — housing analysts continue to flag the lack of measurable benchmarks.
Sources: Housing, Infrastructure and Communities Canada, January 2026; Housing, Infrastructure and Communities Canada, July 2026; Policy Options, May 2026; Build Canada Homes, Manitoba/Winnipeg partnership, September 8, 2026; CBC News, September 8, 2026; Build Canada Homes, Alberta partnership, September 9, 2026; Global News, September 9, 2026; Build Canada Homes, Canada Rental Protection Fund launch, September 22, 2026.
Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship CanadaTabled November 4, 2025
2026–2028 Immigration Levels Plan — cut temporary resident admissions, hold permanent resident admissions near current levels.
  • November 4, 2025Plan tabled: permanent resident admissions set at 380,000/year through 2028; new temporary resident arrivals targeted at 385,000 in 2026, then 370,000 in 2027–28 (down from 367,750 work permits and 305,900 study permits in 2025); explicit goal of temporary residents under 5% of the population by end of 2027.
  • Target: early–mid 20272026's actual admission numbers won't be confirmed against the plan's targets until IRCC publishes year-end figures. As of late 2025, temporary residents stood near 7% of the population, above the <5%-by-2027 goal.
Source: IRCC, Supplementary Information for the 2026–2028 Immigration Levels Plan.
